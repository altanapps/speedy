Powell holds the line as Tesla stumbles and crypto roars back

By M. Levine · 6 min read · April 24, 2026

The Federal Reserve kept rates unchanged at its April meeting, but Chair Jerome Powell signaled a softer tone on inflation, sending equity futures higher in after-hours trading. Traders now price a 71% probability of a June rate cut, up from 54% a week ago.

The optimism didn't reach Austin. Tesla reported its weakest Cybertruck deliveries quarter since launch — 38,400 units against a 52,000 estimate — and shares slid 6% before the call. Elon Musk, on the call, blamed "regulatory drag" in three states, but analysts on the line pressed him on whether the production ramp had ever been real.

"The Cybertruck is not a niche product, it is the product. If it is missing by 25%, we are not a growth company this year." — Pierre Ferragu, New Street

Meanwhile Bitcoin punched back above $94,000 for the first time since March, with funding rates flipping positive across perpetual futures markets. Nvidia closed at an all-time high after a Reuters scoop that hyperscaler orders for the Blackwell B200 are now booked through Q1 2027.

In Washington, Donald Trump told reporters he would "look very seriously" at a pardon for a list of January 6 defendants, reigniting prediction-market activity on Polymarket where odds of a presidential pardon by July moved from 41¢ to 67¢ in two hours.

Oil drifted lower on weak China PMI data, with crude down 0.8% to $84.20. The dollar firmed against the yen, and the 10-year held at 4.31%.

The takeaway: a market that wants to believe — in cuts, in chips, in coins — but is running out of patience with stories that don't ship.
